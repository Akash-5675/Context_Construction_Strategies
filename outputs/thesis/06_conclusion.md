# 6 Conclusion and Future Work

## 6.1 Summary of the Work

This thesis set out to test whether the chunking conventions inherited from general-purpose RAG hold on biomedical abstracts. It built a corpus of 981 PubMed abstracts across five disease areas and a set of 100 question–answer pairs; implemented fifteen chunking conditions — twelve fixed windows crossing three sizes with four overlap ratios, two sentence-boundary segmenters and one semantic chunker — under a common interface; embedded each with a general-purpose and a biomedical encoder; and evaluated every question under every combination of condition, encoder and retrieval depth, for 12,000 runs with the generator held constant. Three hypotheses were stated in advance and tested with analysis of variance, Tukey contrasts and analysis of covariance.

All three were supported, two of them as null results and one with a refinement. Overlap between chunks had no effect on any outcome at any window size (H1). NLTK sentence segmentation, scispaCy biomedical segmentation and embedding-similarity semantic chunking were statistically indistinguishable, with and without controlling for context volume (H2). Answer quality declined with context size, but continuously from the smallest contexts tested rather than at a threshold (H3). The largest effect in the study was one that had not been hypothesised: window size, with 128-word windows outperforming 256- and 512-word windows on every quality metric in every topic. The biomedical encoder did not outperform the general-purpose one, and there was no interaction between encoder and chunking condition.

Two findings shape how the rest should be read. First, 62.6% of runs ended in a grounded refusal, and refusals tracked retrieval misses; the study therefore reports answer rate and conditional answer quality as separate outcomes and finds that they move in opposite directions as context grows — more context makes the generator more willing to answer and less accurate when it does. Second, because abstracts average 238 words, every strategy except the 128-word window operates at whole-abstract granularity; the segmentation strategies tied because they produce the same segmentation, and the small window won because it is the only condition that retrieves sub-abstract units.

## 6.2 Contributions

The thesis has delivered the contributions set out in Section 1.7.

It provides the first systematic, multi-metric, pre-registered comparison of chunking strategies on PubMed abstracts, at a scale — 800 runs per condition — that gives its null results evidential weight. It provides the first head-to-head test of NLTK against scispaCy segmentation inside a RAG pipeline on biomedical text, together with the direct measurement that explains the result: the two tokenisers agree on 97% of chunks. It provides the first test of chunking × embedding interaction on biomedical text, and the finding of no interaction means its chunking recommendations hold regardless of encoder. It refines the context-cliff hypothesis into a continuous-decline finding and separates the willingness and accuracy components that an averaged metric conflates. And it does all of this on a CPU-only laptop and free-tier inference, with every artefact released, so that the experiment can be repeated by anyone with the same constraints.

The practical recommendation is short: on short biomedical documents, use small fixed windows, omit overlap, retrieve few chunks, and verify rather than assume that domain-specific tokenisers and encoders help. Each of these is a recommendation about what *not* to add, and each saves the practitioner a cost that the evidence says would not have been repaid.

## 6.3 Limitations

The limitations are stated in full in Section 5.6 and are summarised here so that the conclusions are read at their proper scope. The findings are about PubMed abstracts and should not be extended to full-text articles or clinical notes, on which the strategies would produce different segmentations. They are about one generator, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, and the willingness–accuracy trade-off in particular may be model-specific. The remote inference service introduced run-to-run noise despite temperature zero, with a measured floor of about 0.013 ROUGE-L, below which condition differences cannot be interpreted. Retrieval recall is measured against a single automatically assigned gold source per question and understates retrieval success on a corpus where many abstracts state the same fact. ROUGE-L measures surface agreement with one reference phrasing, not clinical correctness, and no human evaluation was performed. The question set is small and was constructed by the author rather than by clinicians.

## 6.4 Future Work

Five extensions follow directly from the limitations, in rough order of value.

**A second generator.** Every finding about the generator's response to context is a finding about one model. Repeating a subset of the design — the twelve fixed-window conditions at two depths, say — with a second generator of different size or family would establish whether the continuous decline and the willingness–accuracy trade-off are properties of RAG or of Llama 3.1 8B. On free-tier inference this is a matter of days. It is the single extension that would most strengthen the thesis's claims.

**Local inference with fixed seeds.** The serving non-determinism discovered during analysis could be eliminated by running the same weights locally with a fixed random seed. This would lower the noise floor and allow smaller condition differences to be resolved; it would also make the experiment exactly reproducible rather than reproducible up to serving variation.

**Pooled relevance annotation.** Marking every abstract that answers a question as relevant — by pooling the retrieved sets across all conditions and judging each pooled abstract once — would repair the retrieval-recall metric at a cost of a few hundred judgements. With a repaired metric, the relationship between retrieval and generation could be studied properly, and rank-sensitive measures such as MRR and nDCG could be reported.

**Full-text articles.** The whole-abstract effect that dominates the results would not occur on documents of several thousand words. Repeating the segmentation comparison on PubMed Central full text would test whether sentence and semantic chunking separate from fixed windows once there is internal structure for them to find, and would provide a fairer test of the biomedical tokeniser on the methods sections where its target abbreviations actually occur.

**Proposition and hierarchical chunking.** The proposition chunker designed for this study, and hierarchical methods such as HiChunk, were excluded for cost. With a modest inference budget — roughly the cost of the generation runs already performed — the proposition condition could be added, completing the granularity spectrum from atomic fact to whole document. Given that the finest granularity tested was the best, the direction of that extension is not obvious in advance, which makes it worth running.

Beyond these, two questions the study raised but could not answer would repay attention. The design cannot separate retrieval precision from context economy as the mechanism behind the window-size effect, because both vary together at fixed k; a design that fixes context volume and varies granularity within it would. And the encoder result — that a biomedical retriever was worse at biomedical retrieval — was unexpected enough to deserve its own investigation, with attention to whether the fine-tuning data of the domain encoder matches the form of the questions being asked.

## 6.5 Closing Remark

The field is moving toward chunking strategies that are more adaptive, more hierarchical and more expensive, on the assumption that better boundaries yield better answers. This thesis tested that assumption on the documents most biomedical retrieval systems actually index, and found that on those documents the simplest strategy was the best, the elaborate strategies were indistinguishable from each other and from a plain sentence splitter, and the only variable that mattered was how much text reached the generator. The lesson is not that elaborate methods are useless — on longer, more structured documents the literature says otherwise — but that their value depends on the text, and that the text should be measured before the method is chosen.
